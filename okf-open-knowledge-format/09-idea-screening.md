## Source report: subagent-summary-0-20260907_125706_457091.txt

Rubric: 1–10; higher is better for scientific value, feasibility, likely novelty, data availability, and validation strength; higher risk is worse.

1. Regime-Aware Monofractal Estimation of Well-Log Roughness — scientific value 7; feasibility 8; likely novelty 5; data availability 8; validation strength 7; risk 4. Useful and realistic, but mainly a careful synthesis of known warnings about nonstationarity, facies mixing, and spectral artifacts. Novelty search: YES, but only for genuinely regime-aware well-log workflows.

2. Tool-Resolution-Corrected Fractal Dimension for Thin-Bed Detection — scientific value 8; feasibility 6; likely novelty 6; data availability 5; validation strength 6; risk 7. Strong practical question, but it substantially overlaps Li & Cheng’s Fractal Correction of Well Logging Curves. Without tool-response metadata and independent thin-bed truth, it becomes spectral sharpening dressed as geology. Novelty search: YES, priority high.

3. Scale-Dependent Fractal Signatures for Automated Stratigraphic Boundary Correlation — scientific value 7; feasibility 6; likely novelty 3; data availability 6; validation strength 7; risk 5. Partovi & Sadeghnejad already used wavelet fractal attributes plus mean-log response for automated well-to-well boundary correlation on three wells. A new scale-window or estimator variant is probably incremental. Novelty search: YES only if reframed around a clearly different contribution; otherwise drop.

4. Fractal-Feature Stability Across Sampling Intervals and Logging Tools — scientific value 8; feasibility 8; likely novelty 7; data availability 8; validation strength 8; risk 3. Best-balanced idea. Systematic degradation of public high-resolution logs can provide data even without repeated acquisitions; real cross-tool data would strengthen it. Contribution is methodological and testable rather than another unsupported geological classifier. Novelty search: YES, top priority.

5. Fractal-Conditioned Filling of Missing Well-Log Intervals — scientific value 7; feasibility 6; likely novelty 4; data availability 7; validation strength 6; risk 6. Rahimi et al. already use fractal models to simulate missing reservoir-property data. Conditioning realizations on local H and evaluating uncertainty is a defensible extension, not an obviously new concept. Core validation will likely be weak. Novelty search: YES, but only against conditional simulation and uncertainty-aware missing-log reconstruction.

6. Separating Geological Persistence from Hydrocarbon-Screening Effects in Monofractal Log Attributes — scientific value 8; feasibility 5; likely novelty 3; data availability 4; validation strength 3; risk 8. The direct premise is already covered by Mukherjee & Roy’s HC/NHC wavelet-fractal study. The proposed controls are scientifically worthwhile, but independent fluid/production truth and multiple wells are hard to obtain; otherwise this merely audits labels generated by quick-look rules. Novelty search: YES for prior controlled/confounding analyses, but do not select without independent labels.

7. A Monofractal Multi-Log Consistency Test for Reservoir Heterogeneity — scientific value 7; feasibility 6; likely novelty 6; data availability 6; validation strength 5; risk 7. Plausible gap, but cross-log agreement is not inherently expected because logs measure different physics. The consistency score could collapse into depth-trend correlation or tool-quality QC, while “heterogeneity” remains weakly identified. Novelty search: YES, priority medium, with a sharply defined null model.

8. Finite-Scale Monofractal Maps of Facies-Transition Roughness — scientific value 7; feasibility 7; likely novelty 5; data availability 7; validation strength 6; risk 5. Reasonable finite-scale QC angle, but close to Ideas 1 and 3 and to the existing boundary-correlation literature. A moving-window fractal map alone is not enough; it needs a quantitative comparison against gradients and explicit boundary-aware validity tests. Novelty search: YES only after deduplication and narrowing.

Duplicates/near-duplicates: Idea 1 ≈ Idea 35 (adaptive homogeneous-window monofractal estimation); Idea 2 ≈ Idea 36 and Idea 25 (tool/probe-resolution fractal correction); Idea 3 ≈ Idea 34 and overlaps the Partovi–Sadeghnejad boundary-correlation paper; Idea 8 overlaps Ideas 1 and 3 through local finite-scale attributes and boundary detection. Idea 5 overlaps the Rahimi et al. missing-interval fractal simulation study. Idea 6 overlaps the Mukherjee–Roy HC/NHC screening study. Idea 7 is the least duplicated but is conceptually adjacent to multi-log coupling ideas.

Recommended novelty-search order: 4 first; 2 second; 1 third; 7 fourth; 5 fifth. Search 8 only after merging it with 1 or 3. Search 3 and 6 for prior-art confirmation, not because they currently look promising. Idea 4 is the strongest modest-MTP candidate; Ideas 2 and 7 are viable if validation constraints are solved.
